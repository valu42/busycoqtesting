# Line 34 · What must get past cell 100 for the machine to halt?

**Line 34 remains open from blank.** The machine is `1RB2LA4RA1LA3LA_0LA2RB3RB2LB---`. Its blank tape reached **135,652,670,312 defined instructions** at sweep boundary 250,000. We proved two sufficient conditions for nonhalting. Each concerns the same measurable signal after the first 100 cells. The outstanding question is whether the blank tape obeys either condition forever. [Full checkpoint and signal record](cut100-250000.json)

Start in A on the all-zero two-way tape. At a sweep boundary the old word consists of openings (`1`), neutral symbols (`2`), and closings (`3`). A closing matches the nearest available opening to its left. The **debt** of any word is the largest excess of closings over openings among its prefixes, counting the empty prefix. The sixth unmatched closing at the left guard reaches the undefined instruction B4. Cut 100 is the cut *after* the first 100 digits counted from the left at the current sweep. Let $R$ be the remaining word and $d=h(R)$ its debt. Write $h_k$ for the debt of the first $k$ digits of $R$ and call $d=2$ a double.

## Two ways to prove nonhalting

Each hypothesis below is sufficient on its own, starting at the actual epoch-1,300 seed.

* **Spatial condition.** On every sweep, $d\le\max(1,h_8)$. Whenever the suffix sends two or more unmatched closings, its first **eight** digits already attain its full debt. This permits some triples and places no restriction on how often a double can occur.
* **Timing condition.** On every sweep, $d\le2$ and successive doubles are at least **42 sweeps** apart. Between them, every order of zeros and ones is permitted. The phase of a double can be arbitrary.

Thus a halt from blank would require a sweep with a new record deficit after those eight digits, exceeding both one and their local debt. It would also require either a triple-or-larger debt or two doubles at most 41 sweeps apart. The two necessary events could happen on different sweeps before B4. Losing a sufficient condition can leave the physical guard safe.

**An unconditional restriction also holds on the blank tape.** Five consecutive groups that each increase the corrected closing deficit are impossible past cut 100. Looking three sweeps back leaves no predecessor for their required 25-digit pattern. Isolated positive groups do occur on blank. [Proof and a physical halting control](cut100_ancestry.md)

A third finite calculation combines timing and a three-cell test. If $d\le2$, a double beginning with `323` is always permitted. Any other double is permitted when at least 89 sweeps have elapsed since the preceding double, including the preceding local double. The first double is unrestricted. This admits mixed histories outside either of the corresponding pure three-cell or spacing rules. [Finite verifier](cut100.py) · [Eight-cell calculation](cut100_window.cpp)

## Why the finite tests imply survival for arbitrarily many sweeps

The [52-phase prefix theorem](fence34.md) gives the protected left prefix $P_p$ at phase $p$, its width $x_p$ between 66 and 68, and a permitted incoming debt $U_p$. If the remainder sends at most $U_p$, this sweep has the prescribed safe guard and enters the next prefix. The theorem starts with the literal epoch-1,300 tape. It leaves four phases with $U_p=0$.

Retain the additional finite word $M$ from $x_p$ through a fixed cut $L$, excluding the cell at index $L$. The part of $M$ after cut 100 is $T$ of length $k=L-100$. Let $z$ be the arbitrary debt sent from beyond $L$, and let $b(T)$ count openings minus closings. Splitting all prefixes at the end of $T$ gives the exact debt at cut 100,

$$
d=\max\bigl(h(T),z-b(T)\bigr).
$$

Matching $M$ with a zero exterior computes its intrinsic debt and surviving opening positions. Each unit added to $z$ erases the rightmost surviving opening. Once these are exhausted, each further unit adds one to the debt sent to $P_p$. This tests the old guard before making any update.

If its allowance is met, prepend $U_p$ openings to $M$. Exactly the rightmost ones needed to absorb the incoming debt are canceled. The [boundary bit](fence34.py) and the resulting survivor bits emit one digit per adjacent pair. Equal bits emit `1`, a rise emits `3`, and a fall emits `2`. Drop the last emitted digit to keep $L$ fixed, since all prescribed headers have length one. The result is the entire next retained word. Distinct exteriors of the same debt have the same effect on this calculation. In a spatial test, all admitted $z$ can be enumerated because $z\le b(T)+\max(1,h(T))$.

For a timing test the state also records the number of intervening low inputs, capped at one less than the required separation. Among states with identical phase and word, the greatest reached age allows every future choice available at a smaller age. The reachability calculation propagates improvements and checks the final table against all admitted transitions. The start age allows the first double at any time.

## Where an attempted proof breaks

* **A gap of 41.** The cut-100 model admits a 159-input history with doubles at offsets 77 and 118 which loses the prefix at phase two. Each single forced update was replayed with the literal machine on a canonical finite completion. The full history is physically impossible from this seed. At offset 24 the next exterior digit must be a closing, whereas this history demands zero debt. The bound 42 is sharp for the stated two-input-size model. The physical minimum sufficient separation is open.
* **A nine-cell version.** Replacing eight by nine permits an abstract history that loses the prefix on its 159th update. Its physical guard debt at that update is one. Each step obeys the nine-cell rule. Its externally chosen sequence has no known single blank-orbit realization. [Exterior inputs and replay](cut100-window-verification.txt)
* **A single changed physical cell.** Change the 101st digit of the epoch-1,300 tape from `1` to `2`. Keep the complete rest of the tape, head and state. The new tape sends a nonlocal double at epoch 1,309 (first three digits `232`), then a local double four sweeps later. Their clocks from the edit are 51,247 and 74,041. All 14 sweeps through the next boundary retain the safe prefix. The literal machine agrees with the timed sweep on every tape, head, state and raw count. This control shows how both the simple 42-spacing and three-cell hypotheses can fail after a one-cell edit. [Literal control](cut100_controls.py)
* **A physical failure of the eight-cell hypothesis.** At epoch 1,340 and raw clock 3,891,404, permute `1231` into `2113` starting at zero-based index 113. The tapes agree on their first 113 cells, total length, counts of all symbols, guard debt and cut-100 debt. One sweep later the genuine suffix has debt one. The altered suffix first reaches two at its 16th digit, with zero debt in its first eight. Each guard stays safe. In a separate control, inserting `11231` after 111 cells at epoch 1,339 also gives a 16th-digit violation two physical sweeps later. Both comparisons replay the complete tapes and clocks.
