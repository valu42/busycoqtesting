# Line 13 must keep opening new pairs if it never halts

**`1RB3RA3RB4LA1LA_1LB2LA1LA---1RB` · Possible halting.** On the blank run, either an odd frontier receives its first A after an even number of Bs and halts, or every nonnegative coordinate receives infinitely many entries. A forever-closed frontier and an infinite excursion on a finite set of root requests are both excluded. The proof preserves the actual phase of the blank source through the fourth cut. It does not establish that any later opening has the fatal parity.

The [existing line 13 analysis](../line13-edge.md) proves the fresh odd-cell trigger, the ordinary one-cell lookup table and the negative-half source. The new results here concern the upstream word that triggers failure, the transmission of finite inputs and the parity of every subsequent tail period. These are mathematical arguments with finite executable checks, without a Rocq or Lean formalization.

## Read the danger from the previous even cell

The [coordinate deadline and raw clock](timing13.md) prove that cell 155 must settle its test before $1.1\times10^{93}$ raw steps. The [new timed batch](pair13_checkpoint.json) still leaves that first A undecided.

Let F denote the ordinary one-cell transducer starting on symbol 4. On arrival at a new even coordinate the first entry is A. It emits B, initializes its odd child and leaves symbol 2. Write the input through the next A as `A B^j A`. If j is odd, that second A emits A to the odd child, which has so far received just B and opens its own child safely. Both original cells finish on symbol 4. If j is even, the odd child has now received BB and the even parent has become 4.

In the even-j case continue from this parent 4. Consume maximal consecutive blocks of the form `A B^(odd) A`. Each emits just B and restores 4. Call the number of such blocks k. There are two possible terminating tokens.

| Token at the parent | What it emits | Number of Bs preceding the odd child's first A | Outcome |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| B | BA | 3+k | Halt for odd k |
| `A B^(even) A` | A | 2+k | Halt for even k |

Here even includes zero. A finite input can end while parsing and leave the event undecided. At a safe completion in either row the parent and odd child both finish on 4, and the first letter sent to the next even coordinate is A. In a failure the virgin B0 at the next even coordinate immediately returns to B3 at the odd one. This accounts for the physical order of the decisive instructions.

Consequently a successful even input word with unconsumed suffix S produces exactly **A followed by F(F(S))** at the next even coordinate, using the ordinary transducers on the remainder. Each successful opening erases its local phase history at its two cells, but retains the exact position in the upstream word where parsing finished. This retained position is what a proof must track.

## A finite request cannot cascade forever

Use blocks of four cells starting at coordinates 0, 4, 8, ... . Their first ordinary input on blank is A. A propagated fatal B beyond the fourth cell is only the virgin B0 return described above. Treat all other exits into the suffix as calls with the verified return contract. For a completed block transition consuming one input letter, let its gain be the number of letters emitted across the right edge minus one.

The [four-cell potential](pair13_potential.json) assigns an integer H to each block state. A star after a symbol means its right neighbor is still virgin. The fresh block has H=0. All other states have H≤−1. After the first right emission H≤−2. On every completed transition from s to t,

```text
gain ≤ H(t) − H(s).
```

The [checker](pair13_productivity.py) enumerates the 142 states reachable under this abstract contract and verifies the inequality on all 273 completed edges. It also replays every edge, including the 10 internal failures, directly on a four-cell strip with the same right boundary. The certificate can be regenerated by assigning each state the greatest accumulated gain from the seed, starting the seed at zero and relaxing the listed edges. Its verification just reads the supplied numbers and checks local inequalities.

Summation gives the bound on a finite, successful word U applied to a virgin block. A nonempty image has length at most |U|−2. For an internal failure at an odd cell the first A after its initial Bs triggers an immediate halt. The frontier has made no calls past that odd cell before this A. The fatal B itself goes only one cell farther and immediately returns.

To evaluate a finite root word, first project its calls through the four-cell block. An internal failure occurs before any deeper work apart from the immediate virgin B0. Otherwise evaluate its emitted word on the next blank block. A nonempty such word starts in A and is strictly shorter. Induction on word length therefore resolves the finite input in finite time, including a possible failure deeper than the first block. This also justifies using the periodic root driver indefinitely under the hypothesis of nonhalting.

## A mixed periodic tail passes through every later cut

The blank input at zero is A followed by `BABAB` forever. The finite-state one-cell table, including the initial child flag, gives these exact initial streams. A period refers to rightward entry letters. The prefix before the repeating word is separate.

| Coordinate | Prefix | Repeating word |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 0 | A | BABAB |
| 1 | B | ABA |
| 2 | empty | ABA |
| 3 | BA | B |
| 4 | empty | AB |

To check each line, feed its prefix first and then feed complete periods until the pair (symbol, virgin-child flag) returns to the same state. Concatenate emitted words in their physical order. Coordinates 3 and 4 are important: the eventual all-B stream at 3 has already opened the next cell during its transient BA. At 4 the period AB contains an odd number of As and a positive number of Bs.

Suppose an initialized cell now sees an ultimately periodic stream with a tail period P containing odd a=#A and positive b=#B. Partition the circular period into a B-runs between successive As, allowing empty runs. Let r be the number of odd-length runs. Then r and b have the same parity. At successive A entries the cell alternates two behaviors:

- An A on symbol 4 is silent and leaves 1. During the following B-run the cell toggles 1 and 2 without emitting. Its next A emits A for an even run and B for an odd run, then restores 4.
- An entire B-run on 4 emits BA for every B and stays on 4.

Because a is odd, the next copy of P swaps the roles at every circular A position. After any initial partial run, two copies restore the complete cell state at the same input phase. Across the two copies every B emits BA once, and every B-run contributes its parity letter once. The emitted period therefore has

```text
#A = b + a − r,     #B = b + r.
```

Its A-count is odd, and its B-count is positive. Replacing it with a primitive period preserves both facts because the repetition multiplier divides an odd number. Induct on the cuts from 4: the incoming mixed tail supplies A to any odd frontier still awaiting that letter. If it is safe the cell initializes, and after finitely much input it passes on a mixed tail of the same type. Finite words are physically evaluated in finite time by the four-cell result. Thus on a nonhalting blank run every fixed nonnegative coordinate receives infinitely many entries. This removes the earlier finite-width alternative.

## What the blank histories test

In the [published 10¹⁷-group blank trace](../frontiers/runs/line13-entry-prefixes.json), the two even parents with a completed even initial gap j have coordinates 90 and 96. At 90, j=2 and the next token is B with k=0. At 96, j=6 and one odd-gap token precedes an even-gap token, so k=1. Both produce BBBA at the child. Every other completed opening in those histories used odd j. The last odd cell, 155, still awaited A at that endpoint. Each completed opening permanently resolves that local opportunity. The next opportunity clock depends on how many true source requests are needed, for which actual prefix phase matters.

There is an exact obstruction to discarding phase. The actual stream at coordinate 10 is the pure period `ABAABAABABABABBAABBABABBABB`. At its true starting position the next pair opens safely. Rotate the identical circle by 12 letters and give it to a fresh pair: the eighth entry halts the first odd cell. Every cyclic factor and both letter counts are identical. This rotated input is an artificial control. It gives neither a reachable halt nor a calibrated probability for the true seam.

More generally any finite, safely completed A-initial root word admits a halting continuation if future letters are freely chosen. At the rightmost odd cell, which has received only Bs, request A if its count is even or BA if it is odd. All preceding cells are mature. From ordinary 1 or 2, at most one silent B followed by A emits exactly the desired letter. From 4, a silent A first reaches 1. Compose these finite instructions backward through the mature strip. Conversely an infinite B-only stream at that odd cell can be supplied by repeatedly applying the same single-letter construction, generating an artificial bounded-width nonhalting continuation. The mixed-period theorem excludes this outcome from the actual blank source. Finite prefix agreement alone cannot settle that source.

## The open test at cell 155

At 10¹⁷ source groups, 155 had received only B and therefore held symbol 1. All its left neighbors were mature. Fix any cut u to its left and start with exactly the saved suffix. A finite-state search processes future entries at u in chronological order through coordinates u–154 and tracks the parity of incoming Bs at 155. Its first A either opens the next pair or leads immediately to B3. Breadth-first search on these states gives the following minimum numbers of entries at u. The two minima allow independently chosen future inputs. They give necessary bounds and physical control examples, not the actual upcoming word.

| Cut u | Fewest entries for safe opening | Fewest entries for halt |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 154 | 2 | 3 |
| 153 | 1 | 6 |
| 152 | 1 | 8 |
| 150 | 1 | 12 |
| 147 | 1 | 28 |
| 144 | 1 | 44 |

In particular, a single B as the **first subsequent entry at 144** brings A to 155 on symbol 1 after 17 suffix instructions. A fatal continuation from this suffix needs at least 44 entries at 144. A word attaining 44 reaches B3 after 592 suffix instructions. These local clocks omit the time outside this strip between supplied letters. Both extremal witnesses at every listed cut were run with the literal transition table. [Search and physical replay](pair13_frontier.py) · [Shortest witness words](pair13_frontier_verification.txt)

One new chronological continuation adds 250,000,000,000,000 genuine source groups. During this batch no cell at or beyond 128 received a rightward entry. Thus the suffix in this test is still the actual suffix at **150,750,937,500,000,003,621,311,950,068,749,360 raw steps**. The 250-trillion-group interval does not resolve 155. No directional prediction follows from the relative lengths of the shortest control words. [Complete new checkpoint](pair13_checkpoint.json) · [Batch history](pair13_batch.json)

The open problem is to show that the actual sequence of parsed suffixes always chooses a safe terminal token, or to calculate a specific failing token at its true coordinate. An all-time proof based only on cut-period composition or on unrestricted continuations cannot distinguish the rotated example. No per-opportunity probability or stopping-time estimate follows from these results.
