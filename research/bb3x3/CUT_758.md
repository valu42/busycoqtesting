# A cut through the #758 halting interval

Let A(a,c) be the two-counter configuration for #758 and let W = a + 2c. Write L = H − 12,000,000 and U = H − 3,403,055. The existing basin check establishes halting from every A(1,c) for L ≤ c ≤ U. Every nonterminal update raises W by an integer between 3 and 9. Therefore an infinite blank trajectory would cross the band [2L+1, 2U+1]. Its first crossing of the central threshold M = 2(H − 10,000,000) + 1 would have mass in [M,M+8]. An outstanding proof obligation is to connect the blank trajectory to that basin or to a halt before it escapes. [Published counter map](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/1RB2LC1RC_2LC---2RB_2LA0LB0RA#A(a,_c)_Rules_and_Timings)

## An exceptional branch that leads into the basin

The published rule (e) is triggered at A(c+2,c) for c = 3 mod 4. **If its input mass is in [2L+1, 2U+1−5,458], taking (e) settles that trajectory as halting.** In fact the following exact identity holds for every such c throughout the larger untrimmed band [2L+1, 2U+1]:

$$
 A(c+2,c) \longrightarrow^{787} A(1,(3c+5459)/2).
$$

The arrow counts two-counter updates. Rule (e) first gives A(3c+8,1). For an odd right counter r, put q = floor((r+1)/4). Whenever the left counter a ≥ 4q+3, the two ordinary bulk rules, (b) and (c), both give A(a−4q−3,6q+5). Thus after (e) the successive right counters and all decrements of a are independent of the initial c for as long as this guard holds. The accompanying verifier checks the smallest and largest admissible c. Both execute 785 bulk updates. After them a is even, r is odd and a ≤ r, so the next update is the return rule (h). The interval of left counters between the two extremal trajectories satisfies each of the same inequalities. The total mass increase is 5,458, of which the bulk updates contribute 5,445. Substitution gives the displayed return and shows why the 5,458-unit clearance suffices.

The same 787-call formula also holds on a much larger mass interval. Starting with D = 0 and r = 1, repeat (D,r) ← (D+4q+3,6q+5) 785 times with q = floor((r+1)/4). Every eligible (e) input with D−6 ≤ W ≤ D+r−6 gives the stated return. Indeed its post-(e) left coordinate is W+6, all 785 decrements total D, and the final coordinate W+6−D lies between 0 and r inclusive. The strictly positive decrements ensure all preceding bulk guards. The annulus endpoints are approximately 1.54732H and 2.32098H.

## A necessary pattern for skipping the whole halting interval

Write $B=H-64,000,000$ for the [extended lower boundary](COVER_758.md). Inside the mass interval from $2B+1$ through $2U+1-5458$, a reset proves halting, and the 787-update exceptional return also proves halting. An infinite blank orbit would have to cross this entire strip using only the odd/odd jump J and the ordinary bulk move L. The first mass in the strip is at most $2B+9$. The first mass beyond its upper edge is at least $2U+2-5458$. Each update gains at most 9, so at least **13,465,381** such updates are required. All involved states have mass less than $2H$ including the first one beyond the trimmed edge.

During a run of J moves the quantity $a+1$ triples each time and starts at 2 or more. Since $3^{293}>H$, at most 292 consecutive J moves fit. During a run of L moves, $c+7$ grows by at least a factor of $3/2$ each time and starts at 8 or more. Since $8(3/2)^{787}>H+7$, at most 786 consecutive L moves fit. With at most 24,982 alternating runs, the longest possible sequence contains at most $12,491(292+786)=13,465,298$ moves. Consequently any infinite blank trajectory would have to **switch between J and L at least 24,982 times** within this one strip, without a reset, an exceptional branch, or a halt. Controlling the number of these switches on the actual orbit would suffice. The general length bound for a single run alone leaves the number of switches open.

The [joined interval and coordinate bounds](AXES_758.md) strengthen this to 5,689,983,720 switches across the larger strip.

## Crossings with a recent reset

For the central threshold M, exactly 745 distinct crossing states are attainable when the last A(1,z) strictly before first crossing occurred at most 4,000 updates earlier. The step bound confines z to the 18,000 integers with M−9·4,000 ≤ 1+2z < M. Enumerating them gives 903 seeds that reach M before another reset or a halt, with a maximum of 582 steps among those crossings. Each of the 745 states either halts or reaches the checked basin within a further 4,000 steps. This enumeration does not constrain a trajectory whose most recent reset predates the entire horizon.

## Why listing every branch prefix is expensive

With odd a and c, write u = (W−1)/2 and d = (c−1)/2, so a = 2u−4d−1. Consider two particular nonterminal branches:

| Symbol | Update (u,d) | Sufficient guards |
|---|---|---|
| J, one (j) | (u+2, 3d−u+1) | (u−1)/3 ≤ d ≤ (u−1)/2 |
| B, two successive (b) | (u+5, (9d+20)/4) | d = 0 mod 4 and 0 ≤ 9d ≤ 2u−12 |

At the single mass W = 2L+1, **every one of the 2¹⁴⁴ binary words of length 144 in J and B is the prefix of a legal trajectory**. These starts have a > 1, and no intermediate state is a reset or a halt. They are arbitrary starts on the cut. Their reachability from blank is unknown.

To see this, fix the word and u₀ = L. Its u-values are fixed by the listed increments. Starting with an unknown terminal d = x, invert the branches in reverse order. A J with input u uses d = (d′+u−1)/3. A B uses d = (4d′−20)/9. Their composition has the form (Ax+C)/m with A a power of 4 and m a power of 3 at most 9¹⁴⁴. Exactly one residue class of x modulo m makes the result integral. Integrality holds at every intermediate inverse too. In the step-by-step composition, every later denominator divides m, and every intervening numerator multiplier is invertible modulo 3.

Choose x in that residue class between the final u divided by 5 and by 4. This interval contains a representative since L/20−2 > 9¹⁴⁴. To check the inequalities, let the margin of a state be min(d, u/2−d). A reverse J leaves at least one third of the previous margin, since the new upper gap is exactly the old upper gap divided by 3. A reverse B also leaves at least one third when both old gaps are at least 20. Indeed (4d′−20)/9 ≥ d′/3 for d′ ≥ 20, and the new upper gap is at least one third of the old upper gap for d′ ≤ (u+5)/2 and u ≥ 5. The margin throughout therefore exceeds L/(5·3¹⁴⁴) > 100. In reverse B the old d is automatically divisible by 4, and the upper guard follows from d′ ≤ (u+4)/2. These margins also exclude a reset at the intermediate step of B. This proves the construction. Determinism gives different starting d-values for different words since J and B begin with disjoint elementary guards.

## A bound on the number of cut states that can bypass the basin

At the central mass M, **fewer than 4.783 × 10¹⁰² odd/odd starts can survive 334 blocks** consisting of one (j) or two ordinary bulk updates. This is less than 4.46 × 10⁻³⁷ of the odd/odd starts at that mass. Every other such start halts or reaches the verified reset basin. Indeed the first missing block results in a halt, a return, or the exceptional (e) branch. In at most 668 ordinary updates the mass grows by at most 6,012, so each return is still inside the basin and every (e) still has the clearance proved above. This is a counting bound on arbitrary states. It does not give a probability distribution for the blank trajectory.

The actual blank trajectory could first cross the threshold at nine masses, M through M+8. On this entire cut, **fewer than 6.218 × 10¹⁰³ states can pass this necessary condition for escaping the wall**. Every other crossing from blank forces halting. To see this, the second coordinate is odd between reset visits: the ordinary branches preserve its oddness and an even reset seed at this scale gives an odd second coordinate on the next step. A reset at the cut is already in the basin. Of the nine masses, five are odd and fit the calculation directly. At each of the four even masses with odd second coordinate the left coordinate is even. The next update halts, resets, or is a bulk move raising the mass by 5 or 9. At a fixed input and output mass this bulk map is injective, since the output second coordinate determines q and the mass increment distinguishes 4q−1 from 4q+1. Apply the odd bound with u₀ ≤ (M+16−1)/2 and multiply by 5+2·4 = 13. All relevant short continuations still have the clearance for the exceptional branch.

For the proof of the odd bound, put u₀ = (M−1)/2 and d = (c−1)/2. At index i of a surviving block sequence write uᵢ = (Wᵢ−1)/2. Block J has the update already listed. A pair of bulk moves, now allowing all four residues of d, has

$$
 u' = u+v,\qquad d'=(9d+b)/4,
 \qquad (v,b)=(5,20),(9,35),(7,26),(7,29)
$$

in residue order d = 0,1,2,3 modulo four. Thus 0 ≤ uᵢ−u₀ ≤ 9i. For a fixed block word of length n, form an interval by applying its inverse branches in reverse chronological order to [0,u₀/2], replacing each true inverse by a real affine contraction. Use Fⱼ(x) = (x+u₀)/3 for J, and Fₚ(x) = 4x/9 for the bulk pair. The possible initial d lie within **14 units** of this interval, uniformly in n and u₀.

Indeed both contractions have Lipschitz constant at most ρ = 4/9. At index i, replacing the inverse for J gives absolute error |uᵢ−u₀−1|/3, and replacing the bulk-pair inverse gives error b/9. Either is at most 3i+4. Their cumulative contribution to initial d is bounded by ∑ᵢ≥₀ (3i+4)ρⁱ = 288/25. Since 0 ≤ dₙ ≤ uₙ/2, clipping the last coordinate into [0,u₀/2] contributes at most (9n/2)ρⁿ ≤ 2 for all n. The two errors sum to less than 14.

Each of the 2ⁿ intervals, after padding by 14 at each end, covers at most its original length plus 29 integers. Their original lengths sum to (u₀/2)(1/3+4/9)ⁿ. The number of possible starting d is therefore at most

$$
 \left\lfloor (u₀/2)(7/9)^n \right\rfloor + 29\cdot 2^n .
$$

Substituting n = 334 yields the numerical bound above. The bound complements the binary-word construction, which demonstrates how many distinct short paths occur among arbitrary starts even though longer surviving paths occupy a small fraction of the cut.

The two results describe different search spaces. A proof that covers arbitrary cut states must handle exponentially many legal prefixes, whether symbolically or through an invariant. A proof using the particular blank trajectory can eliminate most of these states if it can control the time of its last reset.
