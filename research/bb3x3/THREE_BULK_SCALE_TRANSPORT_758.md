# A three-bulk corridor at every power of three

The [three-bulk cycle on the blank trajectory](POWER36_FOLLOWON_758.md) belongs to a family with an exact transport law across exponents. Write $P=3^m$ with $m\ge20$. Even reset seeds in the interval derived below take the opening move, $m$ jumps, three ordinary bulks and an even reset. Its endpoints are $162P/65-2m+O(1)$ and $54P/19-2m+O(1)$. It covers $432/1235$, about 35%, of the gap from $2P$ to $3P$ in the limit.

* For every $20\le m\le292$, every sufficiently deep reset in this interval leads to one specified checkpoint. The [table of 273 checkpoints](three_bulk_scale_transport_758.tsv) covers 12,621 possible exits that merge along 1,403,139 first-return edges. The blank path is known to enter the first 16 intervals, at exponents 20 through 35. At exponent 35 the common reset is 142,194,917,649,902,128. At exponent 36 it is 426,584,752,949,412,132 **conditional on entry**.
* For **every** $m\ge20$, all possible upper exit offsets at $m$ remain possible at $m+72$. At the same offset the next state after two bulks and a jump or exceptional call has **the same right counter**. Its left counter increases by $108(3^{m+72}-3^m)/19$. The larger state then executes exactly **195 or 196 more consecutive bulks** before its next nonbulk decision.
* Fix any old exit offset. Among its states at **any three successive exponents** $m+72k,m+72(k+1),m+72(k+2)$, at least one has a live ordinary reset immediately after that maximal bulk run. The choice of exponent may depend on the offset. This is a statement about a family of starts. A link carrying the actual blank orbit across successive powers or into a halting basin remains open.
* On **any one infinite blank-compatible orbit**, every three consecutive sufficiently large exponents $m,m+1,m+2$ contain an exponent $n$ at which the orbit must make one of two passages. It either takes at least $3^n/(10n)$ consecutive three-bulk returns, or traverses at least $3^n/4000$ consecutive ordinary grouped blocks without any reset or exceptional restart. In the sublinear-ordinary-stretches case only the first alternative is possible.
* With smaller constants the same conclusion holds in **every pair of adjacent exponents**. At one of $n=m,m+1$ the orbit makes at least $3^n/(150n)$ consecutive three-bulk returns or at least $3^n/40000$ consecutive ordinary grouped blocks.
* Allowing the one-bulk corridor near the doubled power gives a condition on **every sufficiently large individual exponent**. The same orbit must make at least $3^m/(150m)$ consecutive one-bulk or three-bulk returns, or at least $3^m/60000$ consecutive ordinary grouped blocks. On the sublinear-ordinary-stretches branch it therefore visits one of two specified corridors on every sufficiently large scale.
* For **each of the 273 archived scales** there is a finite form with no infinite-orbit assumption. The passing exceptional band specifies a checkpoint before seeing the orbit. If blank attains mass $W\ge5.66\cdot3^m$, it either visits this checkpoint after at least $3^m/(200m)$ one-bulk or three-bulk returns, or takes at least $3^m/60000$ consecutive ordinary grouped blocks on that scale. The assertion at a scale where that mass has yet to be reached is conditional.

## The sharp interval and its exit phases

After the opening move and $m$ jumps from an even reset $s$, the counters are $(4P-1,c_0)$ with $c_0=s-2P+2m+5$. Put $n=\lfloor(c_0+1)/4\rfloor$, so $c_0=4n\pm1$. A bulk with current quotient $q$ gives $(a-4q-3,6q+5)$. The next two quotients are $n_1=\lfloor(3n+3)/2\rfloor$ and $n_2=\lfloor(3n_1+3)/2\rfloor$. Three bulks are legal, followed by a return, precisely on the interval

$$n+n_1+n_2\le P-3,\qquad 4n+4n_1+10n_2\ge4P-15.$$

The first guard reserves all three positive left decrements. The second puts the final even left counter below the right counter. All earlier left counters after launch exceed one, since the last is at least two. The initial right counter lies in the range for the indicated maximal jump run. For $n=4i+r$, $r=0,1,2,3$, write $b_r=\lfloor(3r+3)/2\rfloor$ and $d_r=\lfloor(3b_r+3)/2\rfloor$. Then $n_1=6i+b_r$ and $n_2=9i+d_r$. The two left sides are $19i+r+b_r+d_r$ and $130i+4r+4b_r+10d_r$. The four ceiling/floor calculations in the [verifier](three_bulk_scale_transport_758.py) give the least and greatest admissible $n$, denoted $M,N$. Both inequalities are monotone in $n$. The extreme even seeds are $L=2P+4M-2m-6$ and $U=2P+4N-2m-4$.

Let $y=s/2-P+m+6=(c_0+7)/2$. Its increment on each return is $g(y)=m+7+B_3(y)$, where $B_3$ counts the low-bit charges of the three bulks. In residue order $0,\ldots,7$, these charges are $(0,2,1,2,1,3,2,1)$. The resulting graph on $y\bmod8$ has the cycles below. Each even start reaches a cycle within seven edges. An initial odd seed takes its known short return of gain $2m+5$ or $2m+7$ to become even. [Bulk flags](BULK_DIGIT_CYCLES_758.md) · [Odd returns throughout the gap](POWER_GATES_758.md)

A sufficient deep entry interval is $[L,U-10(2m+20)]$, since one even return gains at most $2m+20$. Let $Y=U/2-P+m+6=2N+4$. The possible first even exits from deep entry are exactly $U+2j$ with $1\le j\le g(r)$ and $j\equiv r+g(r)-Y\pmod8$ for some cyclic phase $r$. This tests the last in-corridor even seed. Conversely each listed exit has a cyclic predecessor below $U$. Subtract full cycles to find a deep starting even seed with this exit. A cycle advances by at most $8(2m+20)$ seed units, less than the width of the deep interval. The two linear guard formulas give $U-L>50(2m+20)$ for $m\ge20$ (for example $M\le8P/65+4$ and $N\ge4P/19-3$).

For the blank claims, a previously connected 5,001-seed strip lies wholly inside the deep interval at each of the 16 exponents. The verifier locates such a cut on the original [blank grids](BLANK_CROSSINGS.md) through exponent 31, on the [power-33 and power-34 grids](POWER_CROSSINGS_758.md), on the [power-35 grid](POWER35_BLANK_758.md) and on the [power-36 grid](POWER36_FOLLOWON_758.md). The census computes every allowed exit and advances the least remaining seed by its exact first return, merging equal successors. The largest merger costs 20,347 edges, at $m=292$.

For any two exponents $m'\equiv m\pmod8$, even entrances with the same $y\bmod8$ obey an exact rule throughout their common passage. After $k$ returns their physical seeds differ by $s'_0-s_0+2(m'-m)k$. The phase graphs agree and every half-seed increment differs by exactly $m'-m$, proving the rule by induction. For a single specified entrance, at most 16 evaluations of the eight phases suffice to compute its first exit and exact return count: traverse the transient and one cycle, divide the remaining distance by its stride, then traverse the final partial lap. The verifier also computes the exact raw time. On a fixed seed residue modulo 16 the three bulks fix their rounding choices, every pair of counters along the word is affine in the seed, and every [branch clock](timing_758.py) is quadratic or linear. The [clock evaluator](target_clock_758.py) sums that quadratic along each phase by finite differences. For $m=292$ the lower endpoint takes about $1.21638\times10^{136}$ returns and $1.06520\times10^{416}$ raw steps to exit at $U+28$. For $m=1000$ these are about $2.29393\times10^{473}$ returns and $8.06562\times10^{1428}$ steps, with exit $U+718$. These examples use the specified lower endpoints.

## Transport by 72 exponents

Put $\Delta=3^{m+72}-3^m$. Since $3^{72}\equiv1\pmod{65\cdot19}$, the endpoint quotients $M,N$ increase by $8\Delta/65,4\Delta/19$. Thus $L$ increases by $162\Delta/65-144$ and $U$ by $54\Delta/19-144$. The upper phase $Y\bmod8$ and the residue graph are unchanged, while each increment $g(r)$ grows by 72. The set of exit offsets therefore retains every old member and gains exactly nine more per cyclic phase.

At an exit $s=U+2j$, $n=N+\lfloor(j+1)/2\rfloor$. On its higher lift, the quotients $n,n_1$ increase by $4\Delta/19,6\Delta/19$. After two bulks the counters $a_2=4P-4(n+n_1)-7$ and $c_2=6n_1+5$ **both** increase by $36\Delta/19$. These bulks are legal. Indeed $j\le m+10$ and $|n-4P/19|\le m+10$ give positive $a_2$; both decrements are positive. The third bulk fails by the maximality of $N$. The common shift preserves the gap and the right residue modulo four, so both lifts take the same jump or exceptional restart. Its two outputs have odd left counters differing by $108\Delta/19$ and equal odd right counters.

Write $(A,c)$ for this output, $F=108P/19$, and $x_0=\lfloor(c+1)/4\rfloor+3$. For an admissible exit, $|N-4P/19|<3$ by the first guard, using $n_1-3n/2\in[1,3/2]$. Hence $|n-4P/19|\le m+10$, $|n_1-6P/19|\le2(m+10)$, and both $a_2,c_2$ differ from $36P/19$ by at most $13(m+10)$. The jump or exceptional rule gives $|A-F|\le40(m+10)$ and $1\le c\le27(m+10)$. In particular $3\le x_0\le8(m+10)$.

Set $b=3/2$ and $x_{t+1}=\lfloor b x_t\rfloor$. The cumulative left cost of $t$ bulks along this one right-hand sequence is

$$D_t=\sum_{i<t}(4x_i-9)=8(x_t-x_0)+4o_t-9t,$$

where $o_t$ counts the odd $x_i$. The maximal length $n$ satisfies $D_n\le A<D_{n+1}$. Since $x_t\ge b^t(x_0-1)+1$, $D_t\ge16(b^t-1)-5t$, so $n<3m+10$. Under lifting, $x_0$ is identical and the new left budget is $A'=A+(3^{72}-1)F$.

Let $E=1000(m+10)\le P/1000$. Using $b^kx_i-b^k+1\le x_{i+k}\le b^kx_i$ in the cost formula yields $D_{n+195}\le b^{195}(A+E)$ and $D_{n+197}\ge b^{196}(A-8)-E$. The first is at most $A'$, and the second exceeds $A'$. The integer checker verifies $1003b^{195}<999\cdot3^{72}$ and $998b^{196}>1003\cdot3^{72}$. This proves the increment of 195 or 196 in every lift.

## A live return in every three lifts

The limit $K=\lim_{i\to\infty}x_i/b^i$ satisfies $x_0-1\le K\le x_0$ and $0\le x_i-Kb^i\le1$: each downward rounding is at most $1/2$ and its discounted tail sums to one. All lifts at the chosen exit offset have the same $K$. Put $v=\log_b(F/(8K))$. Under one lift $v$ increases by $195+f$ with $0.08<f<0.09$. If $v$ is more than $0.01$ from each integer, the maximal pure bulk length is $t=\lfloor v\rfloor$, and $D_{t+1}-A>4$. Indeed $t+1\le3m+10$, $|D_i-8Kb^i|\le E$ in that range, and $b^{0.01}>1.003$. The threshold margins from $F$ exceed $2E+4$. A residual left counter more than four below the next bulk cost is less than the right counter; an even left counter therefore gives a live reset.

It suffices that $v\bmod2$ lie strictly between $1.01$ and $1.99$, giving odd $t$. The three lifts are guaranteed to have such a phase because the three open intervals $(1.01,1.99)$, $(-0.07,0.90)$ and $(0.85,1.81)$ together cover $\mathbb R/2\mathbb Z$. The latter two give sufficient phases *before* one or two increments of $195+f$, respectively. This argument applies at every index of the lift family. The different exponents generally describe different trajectories.

For each fixed original exit offset, **exactly half the lifts in natural density** take that immediate ordinary reset. To see this, replace $0.01$ above by any fixed $\varepsilon>0$. The relative error $E/F$ tends to zero along the lifts, so the same threshold argument applies outside the $\varepsilon$-neighborhood of the integers for all sufficiently large indices. There, a reset occurs exactly when $\lfloor v\rfloor$ is odd, since the initial left counter is odd and each bulk flips its parity. The increment $\theta=\log_{3/2}(3^{72})$ is irrational by unique factorization, so the sequence $v+k\theta$ is equidistributed modulo two. Letting $\varepsilon$ tend to zero proves the density. Any immediate halt after those bulks can occur only in the excluded neighborhoods, hence has density zero. Likewise the fraction of lifts which add 196 bulks is exactly $\theta-195$: sum the successive differences, each 195 or 196, and use $n_k=v_0+k\theta+O(1)$ from the same bounds. These frequencies concern each fixed offset across exponents and do not assign probabilities to the blank orbit.

There is also no direct halt of the form “maximal opening, exactly three bulks, halt” at any exponent, for either launch parity. Write $h=3^m$ or $2\cdot3^m$. The left counter after opening is $2h-1$. For $n=4i+r$ the gap after the three bulks is $2h-130i-(4r+4b_r+10d_r)-15$. A halt needs gap 1 or 3. Neither congruence is possible modulo 130: $3^{12}\equiv1\pmod{65}$, and the verifier tests all twelve exponent residues for both multipliers. This excludes that terminal word while leaving other continuations open.

## A passage forced on one infinite orbit

Write $W=a+2c$ and use the [exceptional-annulus argument](ALL_ORBIT_ENTROPY_758.md). Every live counter call increases $W$ by at least three and at most nine. An exceptional call outputs $(W+6,1)$, so its bulk sequence always starts at $x_0=3$ and has one constant $K_E=\lim x_i/b^i$. For $P=3^n$ let $W_*=26P/5$ and define $B_n=[0.999W_*,1.001W_*]$. This band corresponds to reset seeds near $2.6P$, strictly inside the three-bulk corridor. Put $v_n=\log_b(W_*/(8K_E))$. A favorable exponent satisfies $1.03<v_n\bmod2<1.97$. In its mass band, $\log_b((W+6)/W_*)$ lies between $-0.01$ and $0.01$ for all sufficiently large $n$, since $b^{0.01}>1.003$. The cumulative-cost estimate above, now with left budget $W+6$, shows that *each* exceptional call in the band is followed by $O(n)$ bulks and an immediate live reset. The parity is odd and the distance to the next bulk-cost threshold exceeds four.

One of $m,m+1,m+2$ is favorable for **every** $m$. Indeed $v_n$ advances by $\log_b3\in(2.70,2.71)$ on each single exponent. The sufficient initial phase intervals $(1.03,1.97)$, $(0.33,1.26)$ and $(-0.37,0.55)$ cover the circle modulo two and enter the favorable interval after zero, one or two exponent increments, respectively. As a finite cross-check, for each $m=20,\ldots,292$ the verifier encloses the odd mass endpoints and checks that the entire band has one odd bulk length giving a live return after an exceptional call. The first passing exponent among the three is $m$ for 135 triples, $m+1$ for 97 and $m+2$ for 41.

Fix this favorable $n$ and an infinite orbit, which must traverse its mass band. If it meets a reset in $B_n$, this initially has $s\le 2.6026P$. After at most one short parity correction it executes at least $P/(10n)$ consecutive three-bulk returns, for all sufficiently large $n$. The distance to $U$ exceeds $0.23P$ even after the correction, and each even gain is at most $2n+20$. Every such return costs more than $P^2$ raw steps: its last opening jump has left counter $4P/3-1$ and costs at least three times the square of that counter.

If the band has no reset, its central half $[0.9995W_*,1.0005W_*]$ has no exceptional call for all sufficiently large $n$. Such a call would cause a reset after $O(n)$ subsequent mass gain, still in $B_n$. Crossing the central half therefore takes at least $(0.0052P-O(1))/9$ consecutive ordinary calls. Dropping at most two calls for alignment and grouping jumps singly and bulks in pairs gives more than $P/4000$ consecutive ordinary blocks. In particular, an infinite orbit whose ordinary stretches are sublinear in their starting mass must take the three-bulk passage on at least one exponent of every sufficiently large triple. This conclusion concerns the same trajectory across scales, conditional on infinite continuation. It supplies no finite bridge between the blank frontier and a particular terminal seed.

These intervals cover the circle. At the selected center the remaining corridor width exceeds $0.016P$ after parity correction, while the width of the central half of its mass band is at least $0.0005P$. The same calculations give the stated $P/(150n)$ and $P/40000$ alternatives. The verifier checks the corresponding four choices at each archived exponent.
