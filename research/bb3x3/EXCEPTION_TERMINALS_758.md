# Can the exceptional #758 branch produce a halt before the next reset?

Among the reset seeds covered by either search below, only **A(1,71)** can halt through the exceptional branch before its next reset. It halts on its tenth two-counter call. The blank orbit has already reached reset parameters above 71, and every subsequent reset parameter increases. The searches leave open longer exceptional excursions, ordinary halts, and halting after another reset. [Blank checkpoint](JUMP_AFTER_THIRD.md) · [Original map](check_bb33.py)

## Why the parameter search is exhaustive

For a reset seed of a fixed parity and a fixed maximal initial J count m, put p=3ᵐ for odd s and p=2·3ᵐ for even s. Set h=2m or 2m+4 respectively. Immediately after that prefix the counters are (2p−1,s+h−p+1). The initial count is exactly m when p−h ≤ s ≤ 3p−3−h, restricted to the given seed parity. The lower inequality ensures the final right counter is positive, so all leading jumps were legal. The upper inequality says another J is ineligible.

For the reverse-direction search write the E input as A(4q+5,4q+3). Its mass is 12q+11, and its successor is A(12q+17,1). Starting with C₀=1, D₀=0, put b=⌊(Cₜ+1)/4⌋, Cₜ₊₁=6b+5 and Dₜ₊₁=Dₜ+4b+3. Exactly t leading L calls occur when Dₜ ≤ 12q+17 < Dₜ₊₁. Their final state is (12q+17−Dₜ,Cₜ). This partitions *all* possible lengths of the leading bulk run.

Within either resulting parameter interval, order the seeds so that the left counter is nondecreasing and the right counter nonincreasing. Each common J or L preserves these orders and the common left parity. The condition a ≤ c occupies an initial interval and the L condition a ≥ 4⌊(c+1)/4⌋+3 a final interval. The narrow gap between them takes E at odd a and halts at even a. Testing exact integer midpoints separates these cases. Every same-word interval therefore has an exhaustive next-branch split, including both endpoints. Equal-gap E endpoints have a=c+2 and the opposite coordinate orders force them to be the same state. In the post-E search a unit change in q initially changes a by 12. Common moves can only increase that left difference, so at most one q per word can hit the narrow gap.

This order also rules out an entire periodic pattern without the 17-call limit. Between two consecutive E calls with no reset, the complete word of ordinary J/L calls can never repeat later in the same reset-free trajectory. Successive E inputs have strictly increasing mass 12q+11, hence distinct q. Starting after two different E calls, the larger q has a left counter greater by at least 12 and the same right counter. On a shared J/L word the left difference cannot decrease, and the right counters remain oppositely ordered. Their final differences a−c must therefore differ by at least 12, whereas taking E requires a−c=2. Thus an infinite reset-free trajectory with infinitely many E calls cannot have an eventually periodic coarse J/L/E itinerary.
