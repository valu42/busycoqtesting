# Rotation and prefix obstructions for the 24-letter Wily driver

Let $P=222030111101130321301012$ be the actual period immediately after
the first correction. Its repeating input acts on the alternating exterior
$T=(10)^\infty$. The [paired-column criterion](WILY_PHYSICAL_CORRECTIONS.md)
asks whether every first arrival at an even exterior cutoff is 2 or 3.
The two results below delimit rotation and finite-prefix approaches to that
criterion. Its truth for the actual root remains open.

## No other cyclic phase synchronizes with the actual root

Initialize the same exterior separately at each of the 24 cyclic phases.
Compare two processes whenever both are due to execute the original letter
$P_0$, allowing arbitrary and independently chosen numbers of completed
laps. **No nonzero phase can have the same exterior as the actual phase
at any such comparison**, even on the first 15 bits. The same conclusion
holds for all 24 phases if the other process starts on $(01)^\infty$.

To check this finite obstruction, on the first $k$ bits let $C_k$ be the
orbit of the actual alternating prefix under one complete lap of $P$.
Each letter permutes the $k$ bits, so every common-phase state remains on
its initial full-lap cycle. For a rotation starting at phase $r$, its
first comparison state is the original alternating prefix after processing
$P_r,\ldots,P_{23}$. It suffices to test membership in $C_k$.

| First separating width $k$, other exterior $T$ | Excluded zero-based starting phases |
|---:|---|
| 3 | 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 21, 23 |
| 4 | 2, 12 |
| 5 | 19 |
| 8 | 4 |
| 11 | 9, 11, 20, 22 |
| 15 | 8 |

There are just 128 states in $C_{15}$. The comparison uses entire cycles,
so applies at all times. Equality of the alternating register at the same
phase of the original event word is one possible synchronization test.
Equality of selected output modes without equality of the lower register
is a weaker condition that this calculation leaves open.

Two other initially successful roots already separate by their physical
behavior. Phase 13 has first modes 3, 3, 1 and halts. Phase 15 has first
modes 3, 2, 3, 0 and makes a correction. These are alternative phases of
the driver; the verified blank uses phase zero.

## Every finite successful prefix admits opposite periodic completions

Let $U$ be any finite chronological input word on $T$. Suppose its sweeps
all stop at finite positions and each even cutoff it visits has first
arrival 2 or 3. There is a **single cyclic word with two starting phases**,
both beginning with $U$, such that one phase stays safe at all paired
cutoffs and the other reaches a first mode 1 and halts. If $U$ is one
complete copy of the actual $P$, the construction below gives a cyclic
word of 43,790 letters. The constructed drivers are arbitrary periodic
inputs at an empty edge; they need not be generated by a blank machine.

The reason is that every finite lower register can be controlled while
choosing exactly what it emits. At one cell, mode 3 flips the bit without
emitting an event. On bit 0 the inputs 0, 2, 1 emit 1, 2, 3 respectively.
On bit 1 the input 1 emits 0. Before and after the selected incoming event,
use a silent flip as needed to attain the required bit and restore the
original bit. This realizes each of the four possible output letters.
Apply this construction successively from high cells to low cells. On a
register of any finite width one can thereby request any given finite
output word and restore all register bits. Sending a single 3 to position
$i$ also flips just bit $i$, leaving every other bit untouched. Thus
one can reset any register without any emission beyond it.

Take $d$ to be the first even cutoff untouched by $U$. After processing
$U$, reset the lower $d$ bits to the original alternating prefix, silently
at cutoff $d$. Call the combined finite block $D$. The word
$W=222303111030$ has a six-pair return under $Q$, with every first mode
2 or 3, as in the [mixed-sign construction](WILY_PHYSICAL_CORRECTIONS.md).
Split $W=AB$ just before its first 1. From the restored register construct
blocks $E_A,E_B$ emitting $A,B$ respectively, each restoring that same
register. Form the cyclic driver $D E_A D E_B$. A cut at the first $D$
emits $W^\infty$ at depth $d$; a cut at the second emits $(BA)^\infty$,
whose first letter is 1. At every smaller even cutoff both cuts already
have their successful first letter prescribed by $U$. It follows that
one cut passes forever, while the other reaches a halt. Taking $U$ to be
arbitrarily many periods of any known safe driver shows that the common
prefix length in this construction is unbounded.

In particular the all-depth safe set, among arbitrary periodic streams,
contains no nonempty open cylinder in the prefix topology. If a periodic
stream is safe, each finite prefix is regular: its first singular sweep
would arrive in mode 1 at all sufficiently high even cutoffs of the
untouched alternating ray. The theorem then supplies a halting periodic
completion of that prefix. For the actual $P$, it applies to every finite
prefix whose tested arrivals succeed. The question of whether they always
succeed is precisely the outstanding question.

Run `python3 wily_driver_rotations.py` to check the two cycle calculations,
the exact six-pair safe return, and the opposite completions of one copy
of the blank driver.
