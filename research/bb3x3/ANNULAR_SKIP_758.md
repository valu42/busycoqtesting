# Generating exact skip intervals over a whole factor of three

There is an algorithm that can move a #758 reset chain across $[X,3X]$ using $O(X^{0.881})$ evaluations of individual first returns, whenever the chain's returns up to the exit exist. For a certified superset of exit resets, the exponent on difficult queries drops to approximately 0.732. The proof counts an evaluation of an exceptionally long excursion as one call. Its duration in elementary counter updates remains uncontrolled. A terminal result during an evaluation ends the simulation. An infinite excursion can prevent a first-return evaluation from finishing. The bound concerns how many reset origins must be queried and applies to any starting reset in the annulus.

At $X=3^{292}$, eight levels of subdivision give **518 intervals**. Positions where both parities have a certified skip word comprise **83.675454536%** of the integer seeds from $X$ through $3X$. This measures how much of the seed interval has such a certificate. In the still-open subinterval from $3^{292}$ through $H-1$, eleven of the cells cover **43.981366100%** of positions. Starting at the lower endpoint of the widest such cell, the generic algorithm skips $9.4963168\times10^{133}$ first returns with 204 evaluations. That interval is approximately $1.01764757X$ through $1.02045260X$. Entry to any chosen interval from blank is a further question. [Verifier and skip algorithm](annular_skip_758.py)

## Producing the cells

Let an even reset take its first opening move, and let $k$ be the maximal number of following jumps (for odd starts count all initial jumps). At the end of this run, the odd/odd state has $h=(a+1)/2=b3^k$, $t=(a+2c-1)/2=s+4(b-1)+2k$ with $b=1$ or 2 according to parity. It satisfies $h\le t\le3h-3$. The initial jump count is a nondecreasing function of the seed. The interval $[X,3X]$ meets $O(1)$ launch groups: $h\ge X/3$ and, for $k\ge3$, $2k\le h/2$ implies $h\le6X+8$. Extending the annulus to include the neighbors in the same seed-pairs $(2j,2j+1)$ changes the bounds by an absolute constant.

Inside a fixed group follow ordinary blocks $J$ (one jump) and $P$ (two successive bulk moves) to depth $n$. A final single bulk followed by the even-left reset yields an ordinary first-return word. Mark as difficult any interval reaching depth $n$, or ending earlier at the exceptional rule, a terminal, or an intermediate visit to $a=1$. For each parity, the ordinary word fixes an interval of the index $j$. Along its prefix $a$ is nonincreasing and $c$ nondecreasing in $j$. Thus the bulk guard occupies a lower interval. Odd-left jump and even-left reset occupy upper intervals, apart from a possible final interval with $a=1$. The remaining middle region is an exceptional equality or a terminal. Each division can be found by integer bisection using exact endpoint states. Within a paired bulk, split again after the first move at the even-left state. There are at most two continuing ordinary children per node, hence $O(2^n)$ intervals. Intersecting the two parity partitions on the common index $j$ also yields $O(2^n)$ cells. [Monotone endpoint argument](COVER_758.md)

For a word with $j$ jumps and $p$ bulk pairs reached at depth $n$, set $R=3^{-j}(4/9)^p$. The exact inverse equation bounds the width of its seed interval by $6+(9/5)UR$, where $U=O(X+n)$ bounds $t$ and the subsequent mass gain over $n$ blocks. This uses the fact that the first complete ordinary block after the maximal launch must be $P$. At a given common prefix, the exceptional equality or terminal equalities account for at most a constant number of seeds. An early $a=1$ costs at most two as well. Indeed the first bulk can merge at most two neighbors of a single parity, and after it unequal images remain strictly ordered in $a$. The sum of $R$ over all leaves starting with $P$ is $(4/9)(7/9)^{n-1}$. Consequently the number of difficult seeds is $O(2^n+(X+n)(7/9)^n)$. Marking both sites of a seed-pair whenever either site is difficult increases the count by at most a factor of two. These bounds include integer guard and residue effects throughout. ## Exact skipping and cost

On a cell where both parity words return, let $b$ be the greater number of individual bulks in the two post-launch words. A seed shift by $M=2^{b+1}$ is even and preserves every fine bulk residue inside a fixed word. Jumps preserve binary divisibility and a bulk consumes at most one bit. The mass gain at each step is then unchanged, so the exact first-return increment depends only on $s\bmod M$ throughout the cell. This includes all changes in parity on return. Keep the starting seed and count at each residue until leaving the cell or repeating a residue. At a repetition the positive seed displacement is a multiple of $M$. Skip as many full copies as fit, then finish the last partial cycle. At most $2M$ evaluated returns are needed for that cell. The same word translation makes the raw time on each residue a quadratic in the seed, so raw-time accumulation can also be done by summing a quadratic over the repeated cycle. The enclosed implementation reconstructs the reset and return count. [Fine residues](RETURN_PREFIX_758.md) · [Quadratic clock summation](TARGET_CLOCK_758.md)

At most $2n-1$ individual bulks precede an ordinary return declared before depth $n$, so $M\le4^n$. There are $O(2^n)$ cells, and positive gains ensure that the chain enters each at most once. Each difficult origin can likewise be visited at most once. The query bound is thus $O(8^n+2^n+(X+n)(7/9)^n)$. Taking $n=\lfloor\log X/\log(72/7)\rfloor$ gives the exponent $\log(8)/\log(72/7)=0.8921747143\ldots$. Constructing the intervals by bisection costs $O(2^n\log X)$ guard evaluations on words of length $O(n+\log X)$, independently of how long a difficult first return might take. For scale $X=3^m$ this is an exponential reduction in the worst-case count of queried seeds compared with testing all $O(3^m)$ origins. At $m=292$ the bound still grows enormously, and it gives neither a blank halting decision nor a comparable bound in raw machine steps.

## A smaller exponent by charging bulk pairs more

The verifier also accepts separate stopping weights for J and P. Charge 2 per jump and 5 per pair, stopping the first time the accumulated weight $w=2j+5p$ reaches $t$. Every leaf then has $t\le w\le t+4$. Let $A,B>1$ be the roots
$$
A^{-2}+A^{-5}=1,\qquad B^2/3+4B^5/9=1.
$$
Across a binary split, both $A^{-w}$ and $RB^w$ have child sums equal to their parent value. Extending to the full binary tree only increases the bounds, so the number of leaves is at most $A^{t+4}$ and their summed inverse contraction $R$ is at most $B^{-t}$. The number of internal vertices has the same order as the leaves. The exact interval-width and singleton-exception arguments apply unchanged. Every common word that returns on this tree has at most $t/5+O(1)$ bulk pairs, so each of the $O(A^t)$ refined cells needs at most $O(4^{t/5})$ return evaluations. With $D=4^{1/5}A$, the total is $O(D^t+(X+t)B^{-t})$. Choosing $t=\lfloor\log X/\log(DB)\rfloor$ yields exponent
$$
\frac{\log D}{\log(DB)}=0.8806135192\ldots<0.881 .
$$
The checker verifies the final strict inequality using rational bounds on the two roots. This improvement has the same limitation on the cost of an exceptionally long first return.

## Faster enclosure when an entire range of exits suffices

For a proof that aims at a halting basin or a gate with a common checkpoint, it can be enough to recover a superset of possible exits. This changes the complexity. For any bounded-size incoming set of resets, the verifier's `cover` method can enclose the live exits and retain a possible-halt flag with **$O(X^\delta)$ difficult first-return queries**, where $3^{-\delta}+(4/9)^\delta=1$ and $\delta=0.73177394\ldots$. This assumes the queried first returns deliver either a reset or a halt. A spurious difficult origin admitted by widening can also prevent completion.

In the ordinary tree, stop each branch when $R\le1/X$. Its terminal value is greater than $1/(3X)$. The sum of $R^\delta$ over children equals its parent value, so the full binary stopping tree has at most $(3X)^\delta$ leaves and at most that many internal nodes. Its depth is $O(\log X)$. The inverse equation then gives constant width per full-depth seed interval since $(X+O(\log X))R=O(1)$, and the earlier exceptional equalities add a constant per node. Both the number of refined cells and the total number of difficult seed positions are $O(X^\delta)$.

Process pending seeds in increasing order, merging duplicates. At a difficult origin evaluate its first return (or halt) and insert the actual live successor. At any easy cell with an entrance, each continuing return remains within the certified words until it exits. The estimated maximum gain $g$ is $O(\log X)$, obtained from the word's mass charges. If the cell ends just before seed $v$, replace all entrances there by the entire set $v,\ldots,v+g-1$. This preserves an overapproximation irrespective of the selected residues or number of returns. Strictly increasing reset parameters ensure that each difficult origin needs at most one query in the entire passage. The bisections and pending-set operations require $O(X^\delta\operatorname{polylog}X)$ additional arithmetic operations. This complexity is separate from the work inside the difficult queries. A live exit outside a desired gate can make the enclosure inconclusive. If all live exits feed a halting basin, even an earlier possible halt is compatible with the desired conclusion.
