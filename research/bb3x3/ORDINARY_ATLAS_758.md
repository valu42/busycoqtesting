# Exact finite simulation through a long #758 excursion

From any blank-compatible counter state at mass of order $X$, the first halt or the first crossing of a prescribed mass at most $3X$ away can be found using $O(X/\log X)$ arithmetic operations and indexed table accesses on $O(\log X)$-bit integers, including table construction. The output includes the exact raw transition count. The algorithm has a finite stopping point even if the current excursion never returns to $a=1$. It packs pieces of an arbitrary ordinary instruction sequence into groups of logarithmic length. At the large candidate scale a logarithmic reduction still leaves a prohibitive worst-case cost. The [reset simulation](ANNULAR_SKIP_758.md) allows much larger skips when reset words synchronize and has a separate bound on the number of difficult first returns queried.

## Locating a group of instructions

At an odd-left, odd-right state put $u=(a+2c-1)/2$, $d=(c-1)/2$, and $x=d/u$. Use a lower bound $u\ge X$, a group length $n\ge1$, and $X\ge65536\cdot3^n$. The two ordinary blocks are a jump $J$ and a pair $P$ of bulk moves. Their ideal direction updates are $f_J(x)=3x-1$ and $f_P(x)=9x/4$. A jump is legal if $u-3d\le1$. A pair is legal if $2u-9d$ reaches a threshold in $\{11,13,16,18\}$ determined by $d\bmod4$. Outside a band of radius $4/X$ at each of $2/9$ and $1/3$, the three intervals from low to high therefore require a pair, a bulk and reset, or a jump respectively. The exceptional call lies in the band at $1/3$, and the possible halt after a single bulk lies in the band at $2/9$. The exact normalized output of a continuing block differs from its ideal update by at most $9/X$. For $P$ use $(9d+b)/(4(u+v))$ with $0\le b/4\le35/4$, $v\le9$, and $0\le 9x/4\le9/8$ on physical inputs (on a legal pair $9x/4\le1/2$). For $J$ use $(3d-u+1)/(u+2)$. [Group formulas and guards](EVEN_POWER_758.md)

Divide the $x$-axis into cells of width $1/K$, $K=4096\cdot3^n$. Starting at the midpoint of the input cell, follow the ideal directions for at most $n$ blocks. At depth $i$ use the guard margin $e_i=3^i(1/K+16/X)$. The discrepancy to the true direction after any accepted prefix is at most $3^i(1/(2K)+9/(2X))$, since both slopes are at most three. This leaves more than $4/X$ between the exact direction and each guard whenever the midpoint orbit is farther than $e_i$ from the guard. In the definite middle interval, one bulk followed by the live reset ends the group. If $n$ blocks are accepted, their exact formulas apply.

If a guard is ambiguous after $i$ blocks, evaluate the accepted prefix and classify the next actual block exactly. It might halt, complete an ordinary return, or take the exceptional call. Each of these ends the group. If it continues ordinarily, the threshold at $1/3$ can only give $J$, which sends the limiting direction to zero. The threshold at $2/9$ can only give $P$, which sends the limiting direction to $1/2$. These limits are the fixed directions of $P$ and $J$ respectively. At the ambiguous input the actual distance to the threshold is at most $2e_i$. Over the remaining $r\le n-i$ blocks, its distance to the corresponding limiting orbit is bounded by
$$
3^r\,2e_i+9\,(3^r-1)/(2X)<1/100.
$$
This follows inductively by the same discrepancy bound; after the ambiguous step the estimate is strictly inside the required ordinary guard. Thus the remaining group consists entirely of pairs at the zero end or of jumps at the half end. A group that continues ordinarily always finishes its $n$ blocks, even in this ambiguous case. The small lookup table indexed by the initial cell stores the accepted prefix and its ending classification. The exact state is retained when switching cells.

## Applying a word and summing its time

For a fixed word with $p$ pairs, write $M=4^p$ and let $Q,A$ be its linear coefficients. Its exact endpoint is $(u+g,(Qd+Au+e)/M)$. Both correction constants and every fine bulk residue depend only on $(Qd+Au)\bmod M$, by the [single-residue composition proof](EVEN_POWER_758.md). Holding this residue fixed, each intermediate coordinate is affine in $(u,d)$. Its fine branch cost is quadratic, so the full raw time is a bivariate polynomial of degree at most two. Six integer evaluations on a translated $M$-grid determine its coefficients. The checker uses the canonical grid corner $(0,d_0)$ with $Qd_0$ equal to the desired residue modulo $M$. It formally evaluates the chosen fine formulas there without imposing physical guards. Legality of the word on the actual input is supplied by the cell proof. This yields an exact endpoint and time in a bounded number of word-arithmetic operations after table construction. [Raw branch costs](timing_758.py)

There are $O(3^n)$ direction cells. The total number of word and residue entries for all possible ordinary prefixes of length up to $n$ is $\sum_{i=0}^{n}(1+4)^i=O(5^n)$. This also covers the homogeneous suffixes after uncertain guards. Each entry takes $O(n)$ word-arithmetic operations to construct; direction coordinates, table coefficients and interpolation samples have $O(\log X+n)$ bits. Direct-address tables give a worst-case bound on indexed accesses. The Python implementation builds entries as requested and uses dictionaries.

Take $n=\lfloor(\log_5 X)/2\rfloor$ for sufficiently large $X$. Then $X\ge65536\cdot3^n$ and all table construction is $O(\sqrt X\log X)$. Each complete group accounts for at least $n$ ordinary counter updates, each increasing mass by at least four. Short groups can only end at halt, reset or the exceptional call. A reset starts with $a=1$ and a right counter of order $X$; following its possible even-seed opening it must take at least $n$ consecutive jumps. The end of those jumps has positive right counter since $4\cdot3^n\ll X$. An exceptional call produces $c=1$ with large left counter and must take at least $n$ ordinary bulk pairs: after $k\le2n$ individual bulks, $c+13\le14(3/2)^k$ and mass is at least $2X$, keeping $a$ well above the bulk guard throughout. Thus short groups and openings add only $O(X/n)$ work. A possibly even-left initial state needs at most two transitions to align, halt, or reset. In the last $18n$ mass units before the chosen cut use literal calls so that the reported first crossing is exact. Summing the two work terms gives $O(X/\log X)$ on the indicated arithmetic model. This is a bound for one actual trajectory; tracing a set of unrelated hypothetical starts can require additional work.

## Finding the first return or reaching a cut

The verifier optionally stops on the first positive-time visit to $a=1$, including a return made by a bulk pair itself. A jump cannot land at one. At an accepted interior pair its output left coordinate is $2u-9d+2v-b-1$. The safe margin gives $2u-9d>36u/X$, and $2v-b-1\ge-18$, excluding one. Pairs in the near-zero continuation after an uncertain jump have still more clearance. Thus the only possible intra-group visit that could otherwise be skipped occurs on a pair at the uncertain lower guard. The verifier tests its actual output before continuing. The usual bulk/reset ending and the literal tail already expose their resets. This gives a bounded substitute for querying an unknown excursion: halt, find its first return, or report the precise state at the specified mass cutoff.

On one actual reset chain the difficult excursions occupy disjoint time segments. The annular simulator can exact-skip its easy cells and use this bounded query on the other origins. If those queries spend $D$ counter calls in total before exiting the annulus, their complete packets cost $O(D/n)$. One-time table work and at most $O(n)$ boundary/restoration work per interrupted query are additional. Short exceptional/restarter episodes inside a query are charged as above. Raw clocks on an easy cell can be summed separately by the established residue-polynomial method. A set-valued basin enclosure can launch extra trajectories, so its difficult excursions need their own accounting.

## A limitation of accelerating repeated words

An accelerator that requires a short *fine* period has its own quantitative obstruction. If the same word of $m$ elementary calls repeats $t$ times, every copy legal in grouped ordinary motion, the existing [integer-defect theorem](EXCURSION_POWERS_758.md) together with exclusion of [all periodic ordinary tails](FINITE_AFFINE_PERIODS_758.md) gives $mt<2m+\log_3(u_{\rm end}/2+7)$. Thus at $u\le H$ a block formed from a repeated fine word of length at most 64 covers fewer than 420 elementary calls. More generally a tiling that uses periods of length $O(\log X)$ requires $\Omega(L/\log X)$ pieces on an ordinary interval of $L$ calls and mass $O(X)$. The bound concerns consecutive copies of one fixed fine word; it leaves other forms of compression open.

Run `python3 ordinary_atlas_758.py` beside the [two-counter map](check_bb33.py). The checker compares both states and raw times with literal execution at the two thresholds, their inverse images, and both axes. At the target scale it reconstructs eight different 144-block mixed itineraries in six groups apiece. From the two halting seeds $H$ and $H-2$, ten groups recover their entire 307-call halts at depth 32. From the conditional reset $3^{292}+1{,}934{,}340$, it crosses 50,000 further units in the mass coordinate using 654 groups and a short literal tail, matching 16,222 elementary calls. These tests verify the local arithmetic on specified configurations; blank entry at the large target remains open.
