# How a connected power crossing selects its parity

Let $P=3^m$, $z=s/P$ and consider completed reset passages from below $P$ to above $3P$ as $m$ grows. Suppose the largest reset gain $G$ touching a passage is $o(P)$. At almost every $z\in(1,3)$ the limiting frequencies of the two input parities along the connected orbit have a unique value. In particular there is a deterministic choice in a cell where **both** local return words preserve their own parity. It can be calculated directly from the two cell endpoints, without running preceding returns.

For a subinterval $[a,b]\subseteq[1,3]$, the precise statement is that $2m/P$ times the number of observed even resets with $a<s/P<b$ tends to $\int_a^b\pi_e(z)\,dz$. The odd count has the analogous limit. If in addition $m(G/P)^{1-\delta}\to0$, there is a deterministic continuum law for the **raw machine clock** $T$ across the annulus,
$$
\frac{2mT}{P^3}\longrightarrow\int_1^3 [\pi_e(z)c_e(z)+\pi_o(z)c_o(z)]\,dz,
\qquad 3^{-\delta}+(4/9)^\delta=1.
$$
This also applies on subintervals, counting complete returns. Any bound $G=O(P^{1-\epsilon})$ with a fixed positive $\epsilon$ suffices. The hypotheses concern the connected orbit; no such bound has been proved for the distant blank crossings.

## Selecting the weights

At odd resets the normalized launch coordinate is $x=1/z$. At even ones it is $x=(2/3)/z$ for $z<2$ and $x=2/z$ for $z>2$. The $O(m/P)$ launch offsets vanish uniformly on compact subintervals away from the seams. [Exact openings and physical guard errors](LOCAL_RETURN_WINDOWS_758.md)

Apply the ordinary map to each of the two directions. A finite word $w$ of jumps and bulk pairs followed by a return occupies an open interval $(l,r)=f_w((1/3,5/9))$. It gives the short-word cell $(h/r,h/l)$ in $z$, using $h=1,2/3,2$ as appropriate. The exceptional directions form a null set because the total length surviving $n$ blocks is $(7/9)^n$. The intersection of the parity cells is thus open and covers almost every $z$. On a compact subinterval of such a cell the exact machine takes the predicted coarse words for all sufficiently large $m$. The output flips parity exactly when $w$ has an even number of pairs: the opening and jumps gain mass multiples of four, the bulks gain five modulo four, and the final reset also gains five modulo four. The whole mass gain is twice the reset parameter gain.

If both words flip, the two frequencies are $1/2$. If exactly one flips, the preserving parity receives weight one. If both preserve, compare the **left** $z$ endpoints of their respective cells. Give weight one to the parity with the *smaller* left endpoint, whose preserving word already held on the approach to the joint cell.

To prove this final rule, each positive inverse-gap endpoint is an odd numerator over an odd power-of-three denominator (including $3^0$). Indeed, inverse jumps divide by three and inverse pairs send $a/b$ to $(4a+5b)/(9b)$. The even-to-odd launch ratio is $2$ or $2/3$. Thus an endpoint for one parity cannot be an endpoint for the other. No both-preserving interval is cut at the seam $z=2$, since the odd launch there lies in the open immediate-return gap, or at $z=1$, where the odd direction is the endpoint 1 outside all finite gaps.

At the left endpoint $z_0$ of a both-preserving intersection, the spanning parity therefore keeps its word on an open neighborhood of $z_0$. Immediately to the left of $z_0$ in $z$ lie ordinary gaps of the other parity with both possible pair-count parities. In angular coordinates this is the outside of the upper endpoint of one middle gap. Inside the cylinder's right child, take arbitrarily many left children and then optionally another right child before using a middle gap. These two choices give opposite pair-count parities and accumulate at the required endpoint. Choose one fixed such open gap where that parity flips. On a closed interior subinterval, either input now feeds the spanning parity after at most one return. Since $G/P\to0$, the connected orbit visits this subinterval for sufficiently large $m$. The spanning word stays legal all the way from it through every compact subinterval of the both-preserving cell. So the parity crossing that cell is forced.

For example, at $z=18/17$ the even and odd words after launch are $PJ$ and $PPP$. The odd preserving word begins earlier, forcing odd parity. At $z=13/12$ they are $PJ$ and $PPPJ$, and the same even word now spans the entry, forcing even parity. Here $P$ in a word denotes a pair of bulks.

The [reset-count law](RESET_COUNT_TRANSPORT_758.md) gives uniform convergence of the total rescaled counting measure to ordinary length. On compact regular cells the preceding parity rules give the separate measures, with an alternating pair of nearby resets in the doubly flipping case. Exhausting the full-measure union of regular cells proves the stated limit for each parity.

## Integrating the raw clock

For normalized launch height $h$, the leading jumps contribute $3h^2/2$ in units of $P^2$ in the limit. This follows by summing their quadratic costs, including the whole geometric series. The even opening has vanishing normalized cost. Start the subsequent homogeneous word at $(a,c)=(2h,z-h)$. Each individual instruction contributes $3\min(a,c)^2$. Apply $(a,c)\mapsto(3a,c-a)$ for a jump and $(a,c)\mapsto(a-c,3c/2)$ for a bulk. Include the final single bulk and then the reset cost. Their sum with the opening cost is $c_e(z)$ or $c_o(z)$. Each is quadratic on every open regular cell. The normalized physical costs converge uniformly on compact subsets there by the exact [counter clock formulas](timing_758.py).

For convergence of the integral it remains to control long tails uniformly. Truncate the angular tree at cylinder width $G/P$, exactly as in the [transport proof](RESET_COUNT_TRANSPORT_758.md), and write $L\le(3P/G)^\delta$ for its leaf count. Exceptional or cutoff-reaching returns together gain $O(P^\delta G^{1-\delta})$ seed units. Their raw time is at most a constant times $P^2$ per unit of seed gain since each call gains at least four mass units and costs $O(P^2)$ throughout the annulus. Their contribution after rescaling vanishes under the displayed clock hypothesis. The fourth opening group at the upper edge is charged by the same bound. The two boundary-crossing excursions also cost at most $O(P^2 G)$.

For the remaining ordinary returns, each postlaunch block costs $O(P^2)$ and each entire opening costs $O(P^2)$. At grouped depth $j$, the number of reached nonempty prefixes is bounded by $O((P/m)(7/9)^j)$ plus a constant per distinct reached word, by disjoint reset spacing and the interval-width estimate. Summed over the stopping tree the counting errors are $O(L)$. Consequently returns surviving at least $n$ blocks contribute at most $O((n+1)(7/9)^n)$ to the normalized clock in the limit: use this estimate at $j=n$ for their opening and the first $n$ blocks, then sum over $j>n$. For fixed $n$ the terms from $L$ vanish because $mL/P\to0$, using $G\ge2m$. The corresponding limiting homogeneous integral has the same geometric tail bound. Taking $n\to\infty$ justifies passage from finite-word integrals to the full clock.
