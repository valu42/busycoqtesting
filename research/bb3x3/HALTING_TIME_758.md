# How many steps would #758 take to halt?

**About $2\times10^{415}$ original Turing-machine steps if the blank tape reaches the known 140-digit halting reset $H$.** The community estimate for this particular halt was $10^{414}$ to $10^{417}$. The model below estimates where within that range the clock might fall. The blank orbit's eventual fate remains open. Its twin #153 would halt exactly 50,707 raw steps earlier. [Published estimate](https://wiki.bbchallenge.org/wiki/1RB2LC1RC_2LC---2RB_2LA0LB0RA) · [Proof of the twin offset](RANKINGS.md)

The forecast samples short sequences of exact reset-to-reset clocks and weights them by how far each sequence advances the counter. It reproduced four known stretches of the blank orbit to within 0.3%. On a separate, exactly timed trajectory just above $H$, its error was below 0.007%. The latter check tests the arithmetic scale of the estimate on one specific corridor. Atypical long excursions and the residue classes selected on the unknown approach from blank remain sources of model error. The calculation supplies neither a confidence interval nor a probability of halting. [Estimation method and replay](RESET_CLOCK_ESTIMATE_758.md)

## Why the conditional bounds also cover a halt before the direct seed

Let $W=a+2c$. The covered entry set consists of the contiguous halting interval $[H-33{,}533{,}929{,}283{,}358,H-3{,}403{,}055]$, the three further intervals at scales 8, 9 and 10, and the 2,240,541 captured seeds between $H-3{,}403{,}054$ and $H$. The farthest certified entry in this set is $H-D$, where $D=905{,}416{,}090{,}650{,}670$. A blank orbit entering any of these sets has attained at least $W=2(H-D)+1$. The angular lower bound there exceeds $2.10448\times10^{414}$ steps, including the 50,707-step adjustment for #153. [Intervals](LOWER_SCALE_GATES_758.md) · [Boundary classification](AFTER_TARGET_758.md)

For entries in the original inner wall, the proof reaches $H$. The captured upper boundary reaches $H$ or $H-2$. Both paths halt at the same terminal input of mass $V=2H+1258$. In the outer intervals the transfer proof allows an earlier halt. Each segment either enters the next admitted reset set at a strictly larger seed or halts on its checked word. In the original cover that word uses at most 10,000 calls. In the outer gateway the odd prefix has 307 calls, the second jump batch has at most 308, and the final trace uses at most 10,000. The even word is shorter. Every segment that can halt before reaching the original wall starts at a seed at most $H-3{,}403{,}055$. Each continuing call gains at most nine in mass, so even its last input has mass strictly below $2H$. Monotonicity bounds every intermediate mass on successful transfers as well. This gives the same upper bound $V$ on the input mass of every covered halt. [Original cover](COVER_758.md) · [Transfer verifier](gateway_758.cpp) · [Universal induction](UNIVERSAL_758.md)

From an entry seed $s$, there are at most $\lfloor(V-2s-1)/4\rfloor$ continuing calls and one terminal call because every continuing call gains at least four. Every call costs at most $(V^2+30V+195)/3$. Thus **fewer than $2.78\times10^{293}$ raw steps remain after any of these entries**. For the contiguous interval the bound is $1.04\times10^{292}$. In all cases less than $2\times10^{-121}$ of the total runtime remains after entry. Applying the angular prefix upper bound at $V$ and adding the last call proves the stated overall upper bound. [Per-call inequalities](MASS_CLOCK_758.md) · [Basin verifier](basin_clock_758.py)

A halt elsewhere could have a different clock. The terminal-family calculation gives exact suffix formulas even for the next member of the same family, whose exponent is 242,952. There is no established unconditional upper bound on when this blank machine would halt if it avoids the covered targets. [Other candidate clocks](TERMINAL_CLOCKS_758.md)
