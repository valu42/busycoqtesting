# The missing theorem for #758 and #153

**Both blank-tape decisions remain open.** The present research priority is a
uniform mathematical argument controlling the reset passages selected by blank.
Further checkpoint extensions and basin counts are supporting calculations;
they do not supply that argument or a reliable estimate of its difficulty.

This note gives one sufficient theorem, the information a proposed invariant
must retain, and criteria for judging the next step. The existing
[joint real and binary-adic coding](SEPARATED_FACTORS_758.md), now accompanied
by an explicit finite-prefix congruence, makes the arithmetic constraint on
an ordinary excursion precise. It is a formulation of the obstacle, not a
proof that the obstacle is absent.

## 1. A single closing implication

Keep the established constants

$$
S_0=150124733170248900,\qquad
H=\frac{3^{307}-8282708212}{13959275},
$$
$$
B=H-160391744210493965542,\qquad U=H-3403055.
$$

The [connected blank proof](post36_ladder_758.md) reaches the reset
$A(1,S_0)$. The [basin theorem](TARGET_GAP_21_758.md) proves that every
$A(1,s)$ with $B\le s\le U$ halts. A reset is a visit to $a=1$,
and a first return means the first positive-time such visit.

**Barrier and closure principle.** It suffices to give a set of integer seeds
$\mathcal R\subseteq[S_0,B-1]$ with all three properties:

1. $S_0\in\mathcal R$.
2. From each $A(1,s)$, $s\in\mathcal R$, a certificate proves a halt or
   a positive-time later reset $A(1,T(s))$ in finite time, with
   $T(s)\le U$. The selected reset need not be the first return.
3. Whenever that selected reset satisfies $T(s)<B$, it belongs to
   $\mathcal R$.

**Proof.** The mass $W=a+2c$ strictly increases on every live counter call.
At a reset it is $2s+1$, so every certified reset passage has $T(s)>s$.
Consequently $U-s$ is a nonnegative integer that strictly decreases along
the selected reset chain while it remains in $\mathcal R$. Property 2
forbids an infinite unfinished passage between selected resets. There cannot
be infinitely many selected resets inside the bounded seed interval. The
chain must halt or reach a
reset in $[B,U]$, whose future halts by the basin theorem. Both encodings
share this future. This decides this pair, not all remaining BB(3,3) classes.

The principle is an elementary sufficient condition, not a newly established
invariant. **No such closed set $\mathcal R$ has been proved.** Taking all
seeds in $[S_0,B-1]$ would remove the ancestry obligation but demand a
stronger excursion theorem. Restricting to selected checkpoints reduces the
required domain but introduces the closure obligation. Neither choice may
assume that every queried excursion eventually returns.

Allowing a later reset is useful: $\mathcal R$ need not contain every
intermediate reset. A gate merger can supply one certified passage across
many different first-return words. The established merger theorems are valid
local examples of such passages. The missing result is a uniform connection
between a sufficient family of them, including its blank entrance and basin
exit. This leaves room for compression without assuming an order-preserving
first-return map.

The current results have different roles:

| Component | What it supplies | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Connected blank certificates | The starting member $S_0$ | A closed family of selected later checkpoints |
| Contiguous halting basin | The terminal interval $[B,U]$ | Prevention of a jump or nonreturning excursion past it |
| Ordinary excursion formulas | Exact geometric and divisibility constraints | A bound or exclusion for the selected difficult excursions |
| Uniform parity-passage bands | A proved mixed-word passage and subsequent corridor for every admitted exponent and run length | Coverage of the complementary phases and closure at the next boundary |
| Exclusion of periodic rule tails | A genuine unbounded obstruction to nonhalting | Aperiodic infinite behavior |

## 2. A useful sufficient escape condition

At an ordinary odd/odd state use
$$
h=(a+1)/2,\qquad u=(a+2c-1)/2,\qquad x=h/u.
$$
One jump is $J(h,u)=(3h,u+2)$. Two ordinary bulks form a pair $P$:
$$
P(h,u)=((9h-5u+e_r)/4,u+v_r),
$$
where the four possible $(v_r,e_r)$ are
$(5,-20),(9,-34),(7,-24),(7,-30)$.

Their ideal inverse direction maps are
$$
f_J(x)=x/3,\qquad f_P(x)=(4x+5)/9.
$$
Let $K\subset[0,1]$ be the compact set generated by these two maps.
Its complementary middle gap $(1/3,5/9)$ is the ideal return gap,
corresponding to a bulk followed by a reset. The integer guard corrections
still have to be bounded. Its other gaps are the inverse images of this gap.

The [exact pullback identity](TARGET_SHADOW_758.md) is the bridge to the
integer machine. After any physically realized ordinary word, pulling a
guard backward gives
$$
h-\lambda u=R(h'-\mu u')+C,
\qquad 0\le C\le14.
$$
For $n$ grouped blocks, the inverse cylinder has length
$R\le(4/9)^n$. A surviving physical start is in that cylinder or at most
$14/u$ to its right. Inside the cylinder, distance to $K$ is at most
$R/9$: its largest complementary gap has length $2R/9$.
In particular, survival for $n$ grouped blocks implies
$$
\operatorname{dist}(h/u,K)
\le \max\{(4/9)^n/9,\,14/u\}.
$$

Thus the sufficient condition
$$
\operatorname{dist}(h/u,K)>18/u
\tag{E}
$$
forces escape from ordinary motion within $2n$ elementary calls whenever
$$
u4^n<162\,9^n.
$$
It also excludes an exceptional restart before that escape. Indeed, at the
exceptional input $a=c+2$, one has $h-u/3=4/3$. Pulling that guard
back gives a point $\lambda\in K$ with
$0\le h-\lambda u\le14+4/3<18$, contradicting (E). An unmatched final
bulk is followed by a reset or halt within the same two-call allowance.
The outcome is therefore a halt or a positive-time reset, not merely a
change to another unanalysed ordinary segment.

For a reset seed $1\le s\le H$, its possible even-seed opening and maximal
jump run take at most 293 calls and leave $u\le H+588$. The exact integer
inequality $(H+588)4^{390}<162\,9^{390}$ gives at most 1073 calls in total
when (E) holds at that launch. Since every live call gains at most nine in
$W$, the next reset gains at most 4828 in its seed. This is already much
smaller than $U-B$.

For a proof built from first excursions, the mathematical task is consequently
**safe coverage of the launches where (E) fails, together with closure of the
selected reset set**. A macro passage may cover several such excursions at
once. A density estimate supplies neither property. The sufficient
condition is useful because it explains what a proof of arithmetic separation
would buy; improving the displayed call constant is not the objective.

## 3. Why direction alone cannot be the invariant

There is an interior counterexample to imposing (E) on all reset origins.
For any $k\ge6$, set
$$
z=3^{k-4},\qquad s=145z-2k.
$$
This odd reset makes exactly $k$ opening jumps and reaches
$$
(h,u)=(81z,145z),\qquad x=81/145.
$$
The ideal direction belongs to $K$: a pair followed by four jumps maps
it to itself,
$$
81/145\ \xrightarrow{P}\ 1/145
\ \xrightarrow{J^4}\ 81/145.
$$
The actual integer updates instead give
$$
(81z,145z)\xrightarrow{P}(z-5,145z+5)
\xrightarrow{J^4}(81z-405,145z+13).
$$
Both bulks and all four jumps are legal for $z\ge9$. The signed defect
from the ideal periodic direction changes from zero to
$$
145h-81u=-59778.
$$
The bounded correction therefore matters even when the limiting direction
is exactly periodic. This family is not asserted to occur on the blank
orbit, and the six-call calculation does not classify every member's later
fate. For example, literal execution at $k=36$ does return, in 80 calls
with seed gain 182. The family rules out an overstrong universal separation
claim; it is not evidence for nonhalting.

Nor can reset order supply the missing closure automatically. Literal
first returns satisfy
$$
F(524)=561>557=F(526).
$$
Both inputs have the same coarse word $IJ^5L^5R$. Endpoint ordering
certifies the guards along a common word, but does not make its return map
monotone. Separately, the [existing residue-cycle analysis](SCALED_GATES_758.md)
has a corridor with one cycle and winding 15: its 15 lifted paths do not
coalesce merely because the corridor is long. A proposed renewal argument
must prove the needed merger or track these classes.

## 4. The arithmetic information to preserve

The [coding formulation](SEPARATED_FACTORS_758.md) keeps the two constraints of an
ordinary itinerary together:

* In the real numbers, expansion of the forward maps forces an orbit whose
  counters grow at most linearly to a particular affine relation between its
  initial counters.
* In the binary-adic integers, the same fine itinerary fixes a compatible
  residue of the initial counters at every power of two.

For a reset origin, a finite fine prefix with $p$ bulk pairs selects one
residue class modulo $2\cdot4^p$. Its real condition supplies an interval.
The residue and interval arise from the **same word**. Combining an
independently possible angle with independently possible residues discards
this correlation; an [actual blank-reachable example](ANGULAR_758.md)
already shows that such an abstraction can invent an infinite path.

There is also a precise limitation to the congruence part. For a fixed coarse
word in jumps and bulk pairs, every initial reset seed has a unique formal
choice of the pair residues. After $p$ pairs, the $4^p$ fine choices
give all $4^p$ seed classes of the chosen parity modulo $2\cdot4^p$.
Thus congruence consistency alone eliminates no coarse word for a given
seed. Retaining a correlated aggregate, such as the total mass gain, may
still restrict the allowed fine lifts; the obstruction is to admitting all
of them independently. The real constraint and physical guards must do
additional work.

This formulation does not claim that all rational points in $K$ have
periodic addresses. The known rational-address theorems cited in
[the entry-gap note](entry_transport_gap_758.md) do not cover the multiplier
$4/9$. The ordinary coding formulas also do not settle the exceptional
restart: a full excursion invariant must handle
every restart or prove it cannot occur on the relevant stretch.

## 5. A proved uniform passage, and its precise remaining gap

The [uniform parity-passage theorem](UNIFORM_PARITY_PASSAGE_758.md) now gives
a positive family result at the first change of active parity. Put
$P=3^m$, $r=(3/2)^b$, and require
$$
m\ge20,\qquad b\ge3,\qquad b\equiv3\pmod4,\qquad P/r\ge1000.
$$
For every such pair, an explicit source interval of width at least
$21P/(2000r)-7\ge7/2$ is proved to reach an even reset in
$$
[E_m,E_m+2m+20],\qquad
E_m=\lfloor54P/49\rfloor-2m-504.
$$
Odd sources execute $J^mL^6JL^bR$. Both source parities then use the
even-preserving word $IJ^{m-1}L^2JLR$ until this exit strip is reached.
The proof retains the common six carry bits in the mass and right counter,
bounds the later bulk run uniformly, proves all physical guards and excludes
an intermediate reset. Positive bounded gains prove termination across the
remaining corridor. This is a passage through arbitrarily long words and
arbitrarily many intermediate returns, not an exponent census.

There is also an algebraic entrance result. For every $m\ge2200$, the
source bands capture a lifted path from each directed residue cycle of the
preceding odd corridor. The entrance can be chosen in
$[\lfloor24P/23\rfloor,\lfloor24P/23\rfloor+2m+34]$. This does not say
that every lift is captured. The threshold 2200 is beyond the scale of the
fixed target $H$; this corollary establishes a uniform mechanism, not an
additional entrance along the present blank-to-$H$ route. At the smaller
exponents used in the existing blank research, selected examples of the
admitted bands intersect the old finite
phase enclosures; those enclosing receipts do not identify the actual blank
phase. No new blank checkpoint follows from this theorem.

The [exact carry coordinate](RETURN_PREFIX_758.md#a-bulk-coordinate-that-retains-the-carries)
supplies a reusable way to attempt further cases. With $E=(c+7)/2$ and
$T(E)=3\lceil E/2\rceil$, it has
$$
\Psi(T(E))=\frac32\Psi(E),\qquad 0<\Psi(E)-E<3.
$$
After $t$ carry evaluations, a rational enclosure has width $3(2/3)^t$.
A proved phase inequality then certifies a whole $L^bR$ passage, its output
parity and an output interval of width at most $2b$. At fixed relative
distance from the phase boundaries, the necessary number of evaluated
carries is independent of $b$. Exact arithmetic still uses numbers whose
size grows with the parameters. The certificate is sufficient: failure
means inconclusive, not a nonreturning orbit.

This representation also makes a limitation precise. In a fixed residue
class modulo $M$, the closure of the possible corrections $\Psi(E)-E$ has
width exactly $3(2/3)^{\nu_2(M)}$, even on arbitrarily large inputs.
A fixed congruence lookup cannot give the exact normalization. Correlated
intervals, adaptive precision and aggregate carry information remain
available; the new passage proof is a positive use of those options.

Two composition obligations remain even at this first seam:

1. Prove that the selected entering phase belongs to an admitted band, or
   give terminating passage rules for its complementary phases. Having a
   captured path on each residue cycle does not establish this for every
   integer lift or for the blank-selected lift.
2. Continue every selected exit in the strip $[E_m,E_m+2m+20]$ into another
   certified family. The existing finite mergers at that boundary do not
   yet give this implication for every exponent.

To apply the barrier principle, the complete collection of passage families
must contain $S_0$ and preserve the upper bound $U$ from Section 1. The present
result supplies one local component; its proof does not yet establish that
closed collection. Resolving this first seam alone would not connect the
whole remaining route to the basin.

## 6. Research priority and acceptance criteria

The next mathematical objective is a **uniform family argument** supplying
properties 2 and 3 of the barrier principle. A candidate representation should
retain the correlated real interval, residue information and physical guards,
and prove its behavior under a whole family of mixed jump/bulk itineraries.
It may use a finite set of symbolic cases or a well-founded induction whose
parameters and bounds are proved. A list with one new case for every observed
return word does not supply this closure.

The useful standard for further progress is a proved family lemma that contains a
relevant entering reset or exit class, covers all its admitted parameters,
and leads to a specified subsequent family or to the halting basin. It may
skip many intermediate resets. Its termination argument must cover a passage
that might otherwise never finish. The parity-passage theorem supplies this
kind of component for the admitted bands; it still needs the composition
obligations above. The exact coding supplies a language for attempting more
components, while a successful argument
must explain why the retained constraints exclude the forbidden exits,
rather than merely restate those exits in different coordinates.

Small exact computations have three clear roles: falsifying a proposed
invariant, checking algebra against the original transition map, and checking
finite base cases left by a proved induction. Additional large enumerations
should have an explicit place in a closing theorem before they are run.

Run `python3 decode_factors_758.py` in this directory to audit the displayed
escape constants, reset-order counterexample, interior family examples and
finite coding formulas. The parameterized arguments above are algebraic;
these checks do not replace their proofs. The new passage and carry arguments
have focused checkers in `uniform_parity_passage_758.py` and
`bulk_carry_758.py` respectively.

Future progress reports should state the exact missing implication, what was
proved about it, the range of parameters covered, and any remaining
assumption. A larger checkpoint or basin should be reported as finite
certificate progress. There is currently no defensible proof ETA or
percentage complete.

## 7. An exact target for a full boundedness certificate

The [global safety formulation](GLOBAL_SAFETY_758.md) provides another way
to state the complete closing obligation. It retains every physical live
continuation as one- or two-call macros, with separate predicates for reset
seeds and ordinary odd/odd states. Its source is the known blank reset
$S_0$; resets in $[B,U]$ are successful stops by the existing basin theorem.
The predicate `Bad` records a live macro endpoint with mass coordinate
greater than $U$. No crossing destination is discarded.

A proposed inductive model must contain the source, preserve all live
successors outside the certified stops, and exclude `Bad`. Since every
macro strictly increases the integer mass coordinate, a checked model
would force a terminal halt or basin entry. It would also exclude an
infinite excursion with no further reset. Terminal two-call halts are
successful stops even if their final intermediate scalar state exceeds
the cap; the stated safety property concerns live macro endpoints.

The optional strengthening adds a proved provenance predicate correlating
the ternary residues of the two coordinates, and sufficient carry-based
passages whose entire output interval lies inside the basin. These rules
do not impose a guessed execution cutoff. They are mathematical inputs to
the certificate search, not an invariant bounding the mass by themselves.

The current full-target attempts, with and without those additions, returned
unknown. A positive small control produced an inductive model that was
separately rechecked against every Horn clause; a negative control produced
the expected crossing. These controls validate the encoding and certificate
workflow. **No global model excluding `Bad` for $S_0$ has been obtained.**
