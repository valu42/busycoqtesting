# Which counter values could the blank orbit have at the crossing?

At the central mass crossing, an indefinitely continuing blank orbit must have **left counter greater than 67,108,863 and right counter greater than 16,777,215** at its first ordinary odd/odd state. Across the larger halting interval, such an orbit must make at least **5,689,983,720 switches** between an odd/odd jump and an ordinary bulk move. A proof that the actual blank orbit violates either requirement would rule out nonhalting. [Halting interval](UNIVERSAL_758.md) · [Definition of the central crossing](CUT_ANCESTRY_758.md)

Put $B=H-11{,}177{,}976{,}427{,}786$ and $U=H-3{,}403{,}055$. Work in the trimmed mass interval
$$
2B+1+2{,}000\ \le W=a+2c\ \le 2U+1-8{,}000.
$$
Every reset here halts. The right counter is odd on the blank trajectory throughout any reset-free excursion in this interval. The only continuing move from odd $c$ that can produce even $c$ is a reset, and the move leaving a reset with even $c$ restores odd parity. The initial blank orbit already has a reset at $A(1,3)$.

## The left counter

Consider odd $1\le a\le 2^{26}-1$ and odd $c$. Work with all masses from $2B+1$ to $2U+1$ so the branch test covers the trimmed interval as well. For a candidate interval of odd $a$, enclose every allowed $c$ using its two outer extremes, rounded outwards to odd integers. The corner with smaller $a$ and larger $c$ and the opposite corner bound every state after each common ordinary move. This uses the opposite coordinate orders and the fact that each move keeps the same left parity across a rectangle. Batch the initial common jumps using $(a+1)\mapsto3(a+1)$. After the batch test the corners until both have an even left counter outside the bulk guard. Each intermediate state then halts or resets. Each jump increases mass by four, each bulk step by at most nine, and the continuing terminal decision by five. The verifier bisects when the corners disagree. The 678,105 accepted intervals give a maximum upper bound of 1,926 on the mass gain. A positive-time reset therefore lies in the halting interval.

The remaining singleton tests have $a=2\cdot3^j-1$, apart from the one value $a=28{,}730{,}581$. For the power-of-three form, reverse $j$ ordinary jumps. Since the final right counter is positive and odd, each inverse gives another positive odd right counter and satisfies its forward guard. The resulting seed is $A(1,(W-4j-1)/2)$. It lies in the halting interval because $j\le292$ and the lower clearance exceeds $4j$. Determinism gives halting at its descendant whether or not this reverse history was the one taken from blank. For even left counters up to $2^{26}-1$, the odd right counter is larger, so the very next call is a reset.

Thus the only possible left counter up to $2^{26}-1$ on an escaping orbit in the trimmed interval is **28,730,581**. Its endpoint words differ across this interval. It too is excluded at the central cut. For $M=2(H-10{,}000{,}000)+1$, the first odd/odd state on or immediately after that crossing has mass in $[M,M+16]$. Pairing the exceptional left coordinate with each compatible odd right counter gives five states. All five first return after exactly **580 calls** to reset seeds at distances 9,998,776, 9,998,778 or 9,998,782 below $H$. [Left-coordinate and central-cut verifier](check_left_axis_758.py)

There is also an arithmetic test for a large left counter at the central cut. Divide $a+1$ by three repeatedly until the quotient $v$ is no longer divisible by three. If $v\le2^{26}$, the state halts. Inverting $k$ jumps gives a positive odd right counter, left counter $v-1$ and mass $W-4k$. The mass bound allows at most 292 inverse jumps, so this earlier state still lies well inside the trimmed strip. The only outstanding small coordinate is $v-1=28,730,581$. For every $0\le k\le292$ the verifier traces each compatible state at the shifted central mass. All 1,465 cases return in 580 calls at distances between 9,998,776 and 9,999,376 below $H$. This test applies even when $a$ itself has 140 digits.

## The right counter and the number of switches

For every odd $1\le c\le2^{24}-1$, an independent rectangle check over the full mass range and both left parities reaches a halt or reset within 844 calls. Its details and replay command are given in the [right-coordinate proof](SMALL_RIGHT_758.md). The upper clearance of 8,000 exceeds the largest possible mass gain, $9\cdot844$. An escaping trajectory thus has $c\ge2^{24}+1$ throughout the trimmed interval. Rule (e) there would force a reset after a mass gain of 5,458. Therefore only the odd/odd jump J and the ordinary bulk move L can occur. [Exceptional return identity](CUT_758.md)

A large odd right counter can also certify a halt at the central cut. Let $T(r)=6\lfloor(r+1)/4
floor+5$. If $c=T^j(r)$ for any positive odd $r<2^{24}$, reverse those $j$ bulk moves. To invert one move, take the specified predecessor $r$ of the current right counter and increase the left counter by $4\lfloor(r+1)/4
floor+3$. This makes the bulk guard automatic. Every inverse reduces the mass by at most nine. Since $r+7$ grows by at least $3/2$ on each forward bulk and $c<H$, at most 786 inverses are possible. The recovered small-right state remains well inside the trimmed interval at the central cut, so its descendant halts. To test a candidate $c>2^{24}$, a single inverse requires $c=6q+5$ and has at most two positive parents, $4q-1$ and $4q+1$. If neither is small, at most one is $5$ modulo six and can continue the search.

A run of J starts with $a\ge28{,}730{,}581$ and triples $a+1$ each time. The final mass stays below $2H$, so $(28{,}730{,}581+1)3^{278}>2H$ limits a run to 277 calls. A run of L multiplies $c+7$ by at least $3/2$ each time. Since $(2^{24}+8)(3/2)^{751}>H+7$, it has at most 750 calls. A pair of alternating runs gains at most $4\cdot277+9\cdot750=7{,}858$ units of mass. One unpaired run contributes at most $6{,}750$.

The first mass inside the trimmed interval is at most eight above its lower edge, and the first mass past its upper edge is at least one above it. The intervening gain is at least $2(U-B)-10{,}007=22{,}355{,}946{,}039{,}455$. Even 5,689,983,720 alternating runs fall short. This requires at least 5,689,983,721 runs and hence **5,689,983,720 switches**. This lower bound applies to a hypothetical escape. Whether the blank orbit makes such an excursion remains open.
